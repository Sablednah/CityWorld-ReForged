# Commands

Every CityWorld command tab-completes its arguments. `/cityinfo` and `/citymap` work for anyone; the rest
default to operators (permission level 2), and each has a permission node a server can hand out — see
[Permission Nodes](Permission-Nodes).

| Command | What it does | Node | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/cityinfo` | What the city plan says about the chunk you stand in | `cityworld.info` | everyone |
| `/citymap [on\|off\|keep <n>]` | The city plan on JourneyMap's map | `cityworld.info` | everyone |
| `/cityfind …` | Find a schematic building, a kind of lot, a street, a city or a river | `cityworld.find` | ops |
| `/cwlocate …` | Find a biome or a structure | `cityworld.find` | ops |
| `/cityworld [leave]` | Visit the `cityworld:city` dimension and come back | `cityworld.teleport` | ops |
| `/cityschem …` | List or paste a bundled schematic building | `cityworld.schematic` | ops |
| `/cityexport [name]` | Write this world's settings out as a datapack | `cityworld.export` | ops |

Most of these answer **"This world is not generated by CityWorld."** in a world that isn't — for example
in the vanilla Nether or End, or a world made with a different world type.

## `/cityinfo`

Reports the plan under your feet. Read-only, no side effects.

```
> /cityinfo
at: chunk -14, 22
context: HighriseContext (URBAN)
lot: StoreBuildingLot (STRUCTURE)
interior: Ellis Bakery
shop: Baker (High street)
roads: 2
nature: 4%
```

- `interior` (or `street` when you stand on a road) appears when the lot has a name.
- `shop` appears on a shop lot, as *trade (scale)*.
- `schematic` appears when the lot is a pasted schematic building.

Handy for working out *why* something generated the way it did — and for checking a datapack: if
`/cityinfo` answers at all, the world is a CityWorld world.

## `/cityfind`

Searches outward from you through the city plan, **including land nobody has explored yet**. Every form
takes `tp` to jump straight to what it finds. The search plans on a background thread, so the server
keeps ticking; it gives up after about 9,600 blocks or two minutes and says how far it got. Searching
the same area again is faster, because plans are cached.

### `/cityfind <name>` / `/cityfind tp <name>` — a schematic building

Matches the schematic's file name **or** its title (case insensitive, part of a name is enough), so the
name a landmark announcement shows works:

```
/cityfind liberty
> Searching for 'liberty'...
> Nearest 'The Statue of Liberty' [Highrise] at x=1520 z=-340  (1683 blocks NE)

/cityfind tp pagoda
> Teleported to the nearest 'The Great Pagoda'.
```

### `/cityfind lot <kind>` / `/cityfind lot tp <kind>` / `/cityfind lots` — a kind of lot

For the generated set-pieces that aren't schematics (a zoo is a whole generated enclosure, not a pasted
building). `<kind>` is one word, matched as part of the lot's type, so any lot works (`office`,
`warehouse`, `hospital`); `/cityfind lots` lists the ones worth hunting for:

```
/cityfind lots
> Findable lot kinds (any lot's type also works as a substring): zoo, biodome, saucer, airship, balloon,
  blimp, fishpond, cornershop, castle, oilplatform, radiotower, watertower, monument, library, museum,
  campground, mineentrance, bunker, farm, park, mall, subwaystation

/cityfind lot tp castle
> Teleported to the nearest Old Castle.
```

### `/cityfind street <name>` / `/cityfind street tp <name>` — a street

Every road in a city has a name — the one on its signs, and on `/cityinfo` when you stand on it. This
finds the nearest road whose name contains what you type (spaces are fine, no quotes needed). Tab
completion offers the streets of the district you are in; streets further away are found by typing.

```
/cityfind street cara samara
> Nearest East Lower Cara Samara Gardens at x=1224 z=88  (903 blocks E)
```

Says **"This world has named roads turned off."** if the world's settings switched street names off.

### `/cityfind city [tp]` — the nearest city (vanilla-land worlds)

In a **CityWorld: Vanilla** world (cities scattered over vanilla Minecraft terrain) the cities are far
apart; this finds the nearest one and the height of its streets. On CityWorld's own land the cities are
everywhere, and the command says so.

### `/cityfind river [tp]` — the nearest river

Finds the nearest river water within about 1,500 blocks, on CityWorld's own land with **Rivers** on.

## `/cwlocate`

### `/cwlocate <biome> [tp]`

CityWorld runs its own biome map, which vanilla's `/locate biome` can't search properly. This searches
outward (about 2,500 blocks) for the nearest biome whose name contains what you type — `taiga` also
finds `snowy_taiga`; the mod prefix is ignored, so `cherry` finds `minecraft:cherry_grove`.

```
/cwlocate taiga
> Nearest 'taiga' at x=-560 z=880  (912 blocks SW)

/cwlocate desert tp
> Teleported to the nearest 'desert'.
```

It works with every style that has CityWorld's climate biomes: City (Modern), Apocalypse, Classic,
Metro, Sparse, Nature. Styles with one fixed biome (Astral, Destroyed, Floating, Flooded, Maze, Sand
Dunes, Snow Dunes) and CityWorld: Vanilla worlds have nothing to search, and the command says so.

### `/cwlocate structure <id>` / `/cwlocate structure tp <id>`

Finds the nearest structure of that id within about 1,600 blocks. The id can be full
(`minecraft:village_plains`) or just its name (`village_plains`), and tab-completes from every installed
structure.

**This is the way to check a structure datapack worked.** In a CityWorld world it only finds structures
that world actually places — the ones switched on by `#cityworld:allowed` or the Customize screen's
**Structures** page. Vanilla's `/locate structure` lists every installed structure whether it is switched
on or not.

## `/citymap [on|off]` / `/citymap keep <overlays>`

With **JourneyMap** installed, CityWorld draws its own plan on the map: each district tinted by what it
is (highrise, municipal, industrial, park, farm...), and the street grid nearby, *including cities
nobody has explored yet*. JourneyMap can only map what you have seen; CityWorld decided where those
roads go before anyone arrived, so it can hand the map the shape of a city you are still walking
towards.

It is on by default. `/citymap` on its own reports the current state; `/citymap on` and `/citymap off`
switch it; `/citymap keep <overlays>` (100 to 20,000, default 2,000) sets how much of it your client
holds:

```
/citymap
> City plan overlay is on — districts and streets are drawn on your map.

/citymap keep 4000
> City plan will keep up to 4000 overlays on your map (about 2000 platmaps of ground).
```

**What `keep` is for.** The plan stays drawn over ground you have already crossed, so it builds up as you
travel; past the ceiling, the overlays furthest from you are dropped (walking back redraws them). The cost
of holding them is your client's — the server never re-plans or re-sends one — so the right number
depends on your machine. With CityWorld installed client-side the same setting is in JourneyMap's own
options screen, under **CityWorld → Plan overlays kept**, along with the **City plan** switch and a toggle
button on the fullscreen map's toolbar.

The same integration puts a waypoint on rare landmarks as they generate (the ones on the world's announce
list — see [Configuring CityWorld](Configuring-CityWorld)), and drops a marker on what `/cityfind`,
`/cityfind lot`, `/cityfind street`, `/cwlocate` and `/cwlocate structure` find, so you can walk to it
without writing the coordinates down. The two sorts land in separate waypoint groups — **CityWorld
Landmarks** and **CityWorld Finds**. Pointing at any chunk on the fullscreen map names what is planned
there: district, lot kind, schematic, shop and interior, including places nobody has visited.

Without JourneyMap the command says so and nothing else changes; CityWorld does not require it.

## `/cityworld` / `/cityworld leave`

Teleports you into the `cityworld:city` dimension, and back to the overworld. It is a twin of the
overworld built by CityWorld — the same plan as a CityWorld overworld, without the decay — so it is
there even on a server whose main world uses another generator. You land on the surface at your current
X/Z.

## `/cityschem list` / `/cityschem <name>`

Pastes one of the bundled schematic buildings with its corner at your feet, whether or not the world
generates schematics itself. Names are the schematic **file names**, case insensitive
(`liberty`, `taco_church`, `gas_stop`, `chayats-bank` …); `/cityschem list` prints them all:

```
/cityschem list
> 34 classic schematics: crops_carrot, crops_glados, graveyard_knife, richards_house, liberty, ...

/cityschem liberty
> Pasted 'liberty' [Highrise] (…) at 120, 71, -340
```

Your own schematics go in `config/cityworld/schematics/<Family>/` — the family folder (`Highrise`,
`Lowrise`, `Park`, `Neighborhood` …) decides which districts may use the building — and appear in the list
after a restart.

## `/cityexport [name]`

Writes the **current world's actual settings** — however you got them: a style preset, the Customize
screen, or a datapack — into a ready-to-use datapack:

```
/cityexport my-server-config
> Exported this world's settings to config/cityworld/exports/my-server-config as cityworld:modern. Copy that
  folder into <world>/datapacks/ of a world of type cityworld:city (server: level-type=cityworld:city),
  before the world is created or then restart it.
```

The name is folded to lowercase letters, digits, `-` and `_`. This is the easy way to move a world you
like from single-player to a server: shape it with Customize, export it, hand the folder over, and set the
server's `level-type` to the world type the message names.

The pack is written to the settings entry that world type reads — `modern` for CityWorld (Modern),
`apocalypse` for Apocalypse, `default` for the other styles — with every setting spelled out.

⚠ **Before 6.13.1** the export always wrote `default.json` and left out the `caves`, `subways`,
`structures`, `cities` and `roads` groups. With a pack exported by 6.13.0 or earlier, rename
`data/cityworld/cityworld/world_settings/default.json` to `modern.json` (CityWorld) or `apocalypse.json`
(Apocalypse), and re-add those groups by hand — or simply export again on 6.13.1.
